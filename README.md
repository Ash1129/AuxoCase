# AuxoCase

Predictive lead scoring, capacity-aware routing and an outcome feedback loop for HubSpot, built for the BrightPath Payroll case: 6,000 leads a month, 1,500 MQLs, 12 SDRs who can work 500, and a points model that under-scores the two channels that convert best.

Every lead gets **one calibrated number** ("62" means about a 62% chance of booking a demo if an SDR works it), a **rank in the current pool**, a **plain-English reason** with the exact words from the lead's notes that moved it, and an **SDR assignment within capacity**. Which leads booked and closed flows back monthly into a retrained model that goes live only when **one person from marketing and one from sales approve it**, with its numbers beside the live model's. SDRs still make the final call on every lead, and every call is logged.

> Status: a working system on synthetic data against a mock HubSpot that has HubSpot's semantics (webhooks that duplicate and reorder, batch writes with per-record status, compare-and-set owners, property history). The real-API client is written from the official docs but has not been run against a live portal; see `src/auxocase/hubspot/real.py`.

## The numbers so far (synthetic data, `docs/evaluation.md`)

Three years of BrightPath-like history, generated to match the brief and BrightPath's follow-up answers (`docs/blueprint/brief-answers.md`), then three simulated months with the system in place at the same 12 SDRs and ~500 touches a month. The "after" includes the 8% exploration slice: random leads that cost a few demos a month and are what keeps the model's labels unbiased.

The 500 is SDR time, not a constant: 12 SDRs × about 21 hours a month ÷ 30 minutes a lead (a cold touch of 20, then the follow-up and the last email at 5 each). The minutes a cold touch and the hours are read from the SDRs' own first touches in HubSpot once there are enough (the gaps between one first touch and the next, and the time in those working sessions; a typed figure wins), the rest are settings on the Leadership page; a quicker lead is more leads worked by the same team, and routing's capacity line, the Overview, the simulator and the cost report all read the result. Sending the two later emails for the SDR takes their minutes out: 750 a month at the defaults.

| | Before (history) | After (AuxoCase) |
|---|---|---|
| Demos booked per month | 172 | **274** (+59%) |
| Median hours from lead to first touch | 71 | **17** |
| Leads touched within 24 hours | 21% | **64%** |

Where the gain comes from, one simulated month per setup, averaged over five seeds (`python run.py queue-limits`; ranges in `docs/blueprint/brief-answers.md`):

| Routing | Touched within 24 h | Demos |
|---|---|---|
| Model ranking, deep queues (3 leads per SDR), no freshness | 15% | 205 |
| + freshness discount (a lead past its first day is worth half) | 31% | 224 |
| + two waiting leads per SDR | 49% | 249 |
| + one waiting lead per SDR, refilled as they touch it | 64% | 274 |

The ranking alone is worth about a fifth more demos than the history's 172 a month. The freshness discount doubles how many leads are touched within a day and adds about 20 demos even while queues are three deep. The rest of the gain comes from small queues, which let SDRs reach the best leads inside their first day. On the mock, "a lead touched within a day books twice as often" is BrightPath's answer 4 built into the generator. On the real portal it is measured: the report estimates the factor from outcomes (0.46 here, against the 0.5 routing uses).

How good is the ranking? Every number here is on months the model never trained on: it is fitted on the oldest months, chosen and calibrated on the next two, and tested once on the newest two, and the model that goes live is the one tested. On leads the SDRs chose to work, it beats HubSpot's points at the capacity line (precision@299 0.448 against 0.375), but barely differs from the service's own channel-aware cold-start table (0.445). The worked-leads number understates the gap, because the SDRs' past choices hide it (L05). On the generator's truth, for every MQL worked or not across six held-out months, the model's 500 a month would book **348** demos if worked promptly, against **292** for HubSpot's points (+55 a month, +53 to +58 across months), 345 for the cold-start table and 262 at random, with 376 the best possible. Calibration holds (expected calibration error 0.012). The exploration slice is how real data closes the worked-leads gap over time.

Ranking every lead, not only the MQLs, would add about **48 demos a month** on the same 500 (+45 to +51): 358 of them would be leads HubSpot's gate keeps out, almost all referrals and webinars. The model has never seen an outcome below the gate, though, so this is phase 2, in two switches, both off by default, thrown from the Next phases page (recorded with a name) or the command line. `python run.py below-gate --explore 0.5` sends half of the 8% exploration slice to plain leads drawn at random from below the gate, scored as they are handed out; the evaluation report and `python run.py below-gate` then show how many of them have matured and how their demo rate compares with what the model predicted. `python run.py below-gate --rank on` adds plain leads to the ranked pool and has the rescore job score the ones already waiting, best first; it refuses until 100 of those labels exist (`--force` overrides). Either way an assigned plain lead moves to SQL, counts against the SDR's capacity, and carries a "below old threshold" badge (`auxo_below_gate` in HubSpot). A portal set up before this property existed needs `ensure_properties()` run again (go-live step 1).

What the data says for BrightPath beyond the scores:

- **The 1,000 MQLs a month nobody contacts are the biggest number in the brief.** On the generator's truth each extra 100 touches a month would book about 59 more demos at first, falling to about 37 by the time every MQL is worked, so the pool is far from exhausted. Phase 3 reaches them without adding SDRs: an automated HubSpot sequence for leads below the capacity line, with a reply handed to an SDR at once, run as an experiment with its own control arm and priced against what it brings (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, "Phase 3"; `python run.py tier2`, `python run.py costs`). Off by default; its switches are on the Next phases page beside phase 2's. Three simulated months with half the leads below the line in the sequence: 10.7 demos per 100 emails against 0.7 for the control arm, but each reply takes SDR time from a ranked lead, so the net is about 20 more demos a month at a 10-minute reply and nothing at a 20-minute one; the reply's real length is the number to measure (`docs/evaluation.md`, "Cost against outcome").
- **Most referral and webinar leads never reach the pool.** 9% of referrals and 8% of webinar leads pass HubSpot's MQL gate, against 49% from the website, because half of them have no title or size and HubSpot's points give nothing for a blank. Letting the model replace that gate is phase 2 (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`).
- **Larger companies leave for missing features.** Missing features account for 29% of losses at companies over 500 employees and about 12% under 300 (loss reasons read into categories, quotes checked).
- **Enrichment fills gaps.** Asked about 394 pool leads missing a field, the stand-in enrichment tool filled 280. The real tool's match rate replaces that number.
- **Faster follow-up makes the live model under-predict.** The channel monitor flags observed demo rates above prediction. Leads booked more because they were touched sooner, so the model must be retrained and approved.

## Run it

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt && pip install -e .
python run.py demo            # seeds 36 months of history, trains and approves a model, scores, routes, serves http://127.0.0.1:8000
```

Step by step:

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `python run.py seed` | 36 months of BrightPath-like history in the mock: the SDRs' cherry-picking, blank titles and sizes, a 3-day first response, messy loss reasons |
| `python run.py sync` | pulls meetings, deal stages and lead status into outcome labels (booked / worked-no-demo / not-worked) |
| `python run.py train` | trains a candidate on 12 months of mature labels; prints precision@500, lift over the points model, AUC-PR, ECE, by channel |
| `python run.py approve demo-v1 marketing "Name"` then `... sales "Name"` | the two-team approval; both → live |
| `python run.py score [--all]` | scores changed leads from the change feed (or the whole pool) and writes `auxo_*` properties back |
| `python run.py route` | hands the SDRs the leads worth most now, round robin, up to the team's reach in a week (no limit per SDR unless an owner's `capacity_limit` sets one), keeps an 8% exploration slice, creates tasks; strong leads unplaced after 24 h go to the manager |
| `python run.py monitor` | the per-channel p-chart of observed vs predicted demo rate |
| `python run.py flags [--limit 25]` | marketing's second look: reads the records of the leads next out, at the capacity line or whose notes disagree with their odds, and flags what disagrees (student at 60%, a budget at 4%, a title the form left blank); the Flagged page |
| `python run.py losses` | reads new closed-lost reasons into fixed categories (quote checked) |
| `python run.py simulate --months 3` | replays months in six-hour steps with the system in place: new leads, scoring, routing, SDR touches at the brief's capacity, outcomes, loss reasons, retrain |
| `python run.py rebase [--exact]` | moves the mock's demo-clock dates onto this year (whole years, so months stay aligned; `--exact` shifts by the span from the newest lead to now, so every age is real) and lets the clock run live: leads age, labels mature and SLAs run in real time. Wall-time logs stay; a `simulate` replay freezes the clock again |
| `python run.py eval` | writes `docs/evaluation.md` |
| `python run.py below-gate [--explore 0.5] [--rank on\|off]` | phase 2: send part of the exploration slice below HubSpot's MQL gate, and rank there once enough of those leads have outcomes; prints what is known there |
| `python run.py tier2 [--explore 0.2] [--band on\|off]` | phase 3: put a share of the leads below the capacity line in an automated sequence, the rest as the control arm; the band once enough of those have matured labels; prints the experiment so far |
| `python run.py costs [--revenium]` | what a demo costs by the way the lead was reached, with the service's own Claude calls metered per call; `--revenium` adds the product's cost from Revenium's analytics API (`REVENIUM_API_KEY`) |
| `python run.py queue-limits [--seeds 5]` | builds the demo's starting point, then replays one month per routing setup (freshness discount on or off, queue limit 3, 2 or 1) and seed in parallel; prints the speed and demos table with the range across seeds |
| `python run.py serve` | the pages, behind a login: SDR queues, lead detail, manager's held queue, model & approvals; `/jobs/*` endpoints; `/webhooks/hubspot`; the jobs on timers (below) |
| `python run.py staff add <user> --role sdr\|manager\|marketing\|leadership\|admin [--owner 900]` | an account for the pages (`list`, `password`, `disable`, `enable` too); `staff demo` seeds the mock portal's demo logins |
| `pytest -q` | the test suite (offline; no model calls) |

**Logging in.** Every page needs an account (`src/auxocase/auth.py`), and the role on it decides what the sidebar offers and what a request may do: an SDR their own queue and its leads; the sales manager the overview, every queue, held & overdue, decisions, the jobs and the sales sign-off; marketing only the three scoring pages, named in its own terms in the sidebar: lead scoring (the model page and its sign-off), leads to check (the flags) and leads left out (the next phases); leadership the capacity inputs and the phase switches, plus the overview, the model page and decisions; an admin everything. Who approved, released or threw a switch is recorded from the session, never typed. `run.py seed` and `run.py demo` make the demo logins (`sdr1`…`sdr12`, `manager`, `marketing`, `leadership`, `admin`, password `brightpath`), listed on the login page of a mock portal; a real deployment makes its first admin from `AUXO_ADMIN_USER` and `AUXO_ADMIN_PASSWORD` and adds the rest with `run.py staff add`. Sessions are HttpOnly cookies, kept as hashes, idle twelve hours at most; five wrong passwords lock an account for fifteen minutes.

**Flagged leads.** A number cannot be checked one lead at a time, but a record can be read against it. Every ten minutes a reader (Claude, or rules offline) takes the leads routing is about to hand out, the band at the capacity line and any lead whose notes reading already disagrees with its odds, and flags what disagrees: a student or a vendor at odds an SDR would call, a budget or a competitor being left at odds nobody would, a title or a headcount the form left blank but the notes give, text that addresses the scorer, the same person arriving twice. The reader may pick only from that list and must copy its evidence from the record word for word; code drops anything not in the record verbatim, writes the note in fixed wording, and never changes a score or a route. Marketing resolves each flag on the Flagged page (not a problem, a form to fix, agreed), the open kinds sit on the lead's `auxo_review_flag` in HubSpot meanwhile, and the page sets flagged against unflagged leads at the same odds once they have matured, which is the test of whether the flags are worth keeping (`src/auxocase/flags.py`, `src/auxocase/llm/flags.py`).

**The verdict under a sign-off.** A manager who does not know ML should not have to read seven columns of decimals to sign a model off, so each candidate carries one line: approve or send back, and why. Fixed rules decide it against the live model on its own test leads: send back when its calibration error is more than a point worse (its numbers cannot be trusted, whatever its ranking); approve when its lift over HubSpot's points is better and it learned from at least as many labelled rows; otherwise send back, since a reshuffle buys nothing. The reader (Claude, or the rules' own wording offline) then puts the rules' reasons into one plain sentence, and code keeps it only if it starts with the verdict, stays under 60 words, uses no modelling word and cites no figure the facts do not hold; otherwise the rules' wording stands. The rules run at every page load; the sentence is read once a candidate against a live model and its metrics, by the same ten-minute job as the flags (`src/auxocase/verdicts.py`, `src/auxocase/llm/verdict.py`).

**Automatic sign-off.** On unless switched off on the Model page: the same job then acts on the verdicts. Candidates the rules send back are sent back, and of those they approve, the one whose picks book the most demos goes live, signed for both teams by "Automatic sign-off (the rules)" with the sentence as its note; the other approvable ones are sent back as not the best of the round, so one version goes live a run and the pool is rescored once. The rows are ordinary approvals, so Decisions shows them like any person's, and the page says what the next check will decide instead of asking. Leadership or marketing switch it off on the page, recorded by name, and the two-signature form comes back.

**The scheduler.** `run.py serve` also runs the jobs on timers inside the server: scoring of new or changed leads and routing every 5 minutes, first-touch detection, outreach and (after a model goes live) rescoring the pool every minute, marketing's flags every 10, reminders and outcome sync hourly, the channel monitor daily, and a candidate model every 30 days. Each run is one row in the Overview's "Recent runs", a failed job never stops the others, and training only produces a candidate; it still needs both approvals. Once both approve, the pool is rescored on the new model, best-ranked leads first, so the leads routing takes next carry the new number first. With Claude reading notes that is about five seconds a lead, so the whole pool takes a couple of hours; `run.py approve` runs it to the end. `AUXO_SCHEDULER=off` in `.env` turns it off, and `AUXO_EVERY_<JOB>_MIN` changes an interval (see `.env.example`). On the demo data the service clock is frozen, so leads never age into new holds or reminders there.

**The SDR works in HubSpot.** When a lead is assigned, the outreach job drafts the first email, adds the SDR's booking link, writes it to the lead (`auxo_email_subject`, `auxo_email_body`) and enrols the lead in the HubSpot sequence set by `AUXO_SEQUENCE_ID`, sending from the SDR's inbox. The sequence's first step is a manual email task built from those two properties, so the draft waits in the SDR's HubSpot tasks to be read, edited and sent. The first email or call the SDR logs in HubSpot counts as the first touch: within a minute the lead is marked worked, its first-touch time recorded, and the SDR's slot refilled, with no click in AuxoCase. The lead page shows that activity and drafts the follow-up or the last email from it; a lead that replied gets no draft. Or the service sends those two itself: with follow-ups switched to automatic (Leadership, the capacity section), the follow-up goes out 3 days after the first email and the last email 7 days after, each drafted from the lead's activity that day and sent from the SDR's inbox, unless the lead replied or booked or the SDR stopped them from the lead page; on a real portal the sequence's own steps two and three send them from the same two properties, written fresh before each fires. The minutes the SDR no longer spends come back as capacity: 750 leads a month at the defaults instead of 500. Replies can be read the same way (Leadership, "Replies: triaged"): each reply is read as HubSpot logs it, and the plain part is done before the SDR opens it. Interested or a question: the answer is drafted with the booking link and the lead comes first in the queue. Not now: when to come back is recorded. Not interested, wrong person or unsubscribe: the lead is closed in HubSpot. Out of office: not an answer, so the follow-ups wait until they are back. Anything else is the SDR's to read. A reply then takes 4 SDR minutes instead of 10 in the cost report and the simulator. Call notes can be written up the same way ("Call notes: written up"): the fragments the SDR types in HubSpot's call log ("40 ppl, on gusto, renews jan, wants demo w/ cfo") become a summary on the lead's notes, which the next score reads, the size on the company, the next step as a task on its day, and the follow-up email drafted from the call; "not interested" closes the lead. Logging a call then takes 1 minute of the cold touch instead of 4, on the share of first touches that are calls. The leads the SDRs would skip can be held before they cost the minutes ("Likely skips: held"): their own skip and junk decisions are counted by trait (seniority, company size, channel, country, a student title, no notes), and a lead with a trait skipped clearly more often than the rest, by enough decisions to mean it, goes to the held queue with the reason ("likely skipped: an individual contributor, 10 of 10 skipped") for the manager to release or leave. The Leadership page shows what the counts would have held of the SDRs' own skips, and of the leads they worked; the skips held come off the cold touch at 3 minutes each. Demos can be booked for the SDR ("Demos: booked for the SDR"): an interested reply is answered with three free times from the SDR's HubSpot calendar, sent for them; a reply that names a time ("Tuesday at 2", "the second one") books the meeting in HubSpot, which outcome sync counts as the demo, stops the follow-ups and sends the confirmation; a time already taken gets new times. The SDR sees "Demo booked · Tue 3 Nov, 14:00 ET" on the lead and the queue and does nothing; booking then takes 1 minute of the cold touch instead of 8, on the share of worked leads that book. Calling instead? The queue and lead pages prepare a **call brief** on request, from the same record as the email: an opener, two or three questions drawn from what moved the score (a competing vendor becomes "when does that contract renew?", a blank company size becomes a size question), the objection to expect from the loss reasons read for companies of that size, the ask with the booking link, and a voicemail under 60 words. It is read, not recited, and checked by code like the emails. HubSpot's call outcome (connected, no answer, left voicemail, busy, wrong number) comes in with the first touch and shows on the lead page. On the mock, buttons on the lead page stand in for HubSpot (send, log a call with its outcome, the lead replies).

**Sending from the page.** The lead page shows each draft as the lead will see it, in BrightPath's colours, and "Send to <the lead>" sends it from the service over SMTP with the SDR's name on the From line (`AUXO_SMTP_USER` and `AUXO_SMTP_PASS`, for Gmail an app password; `AUXO_SMTP_HOST`/`PORT` default to Gmail's; `AUXO_SMTP_BCC` copies HubSpot's logging address so the portal logs it; `AUXO_SMTP_TEST_TO` sends every email to one address instead of the synthetic leads). "Open in your email app" is still there, plain, since a mailto link cannot carry formatting.

**Safe from other sites.** The pages answer only to the host names in `AUXO_ALLOWED_HOSTS` (this machine by default), every write coming from another site's page is refused, and every reply carries a content security policy and no-framing headers. Forms that act on a lead or approve a model carry the state the person saw; if someone else acted first or HubSpot moved the lead on, the action is not applied and the page says so. With the real HubSpot client, webhooks must be signed (`service/security.py`).

**The reader.** Free-text notes are read by Claude for buying signals. `AUXO_LLM=cli` uses the Claude Code CLI headlessly (`claude -p`), `AUXO_LLM=api` the Anthropic API, and the default `offline` a scripted reader so everything runs without a model. Whatever answers, code verifies every quoted signal against the notes, drops what isn't there, and caps the effect at ±10 points; the model never sets the score.

## Going live in HubSpot

1. Create the `auxo_*` contact properties (`ensure_properties()` does it through the API; run it again after an upgrade that adds one) and a private app with the scopes in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`.
2. Set `HUBSPOT_PRIVATE_APP_TOKEN`; point a HubSpot webhook subscription (contact creation and property changes) at `/webhooks/hubspot` and set `HUBSPOT_APP_SECRET` (with the real client, unsigned webhooks are refused), or run `score` on a schedule to poll by last-modified. Add the deployment's host name to `AUXO_ALLOWED_HOSTS`.
3. Confirm the property internal names listed in `hubspot/real.py` against `GET /crm/v3/properties/contacts` on the portal; they vary by portal.
4. Train the first model on HubSpot's three years of history (the last 12 months, mature labels only; at least 60 demos), with the exploration slice on from day one. Set `stale_lead_factor` from the report's measured speed effect, and set the SDRs' `capacity_limit` to 1.
5. Connect the enrichment tool behind `scoring/enrich.py`'s `Enricher` interface (set `AUXO_ENRICH`), and run `losses` on the closed-lost history.
6. Create the SDRs' sequence: step one a manual email task whose subject and body are the `auxo_email_subject` and `auxo_email_body` contact tokens, later steps your own follow-ups, or, for the service to send them, automated emails of the same two tokens 3 and 7 days on (the service rewrites the tokens with a fresh draft before each fires). Set `AUXO_SEQUENCE_ID` to its ID, connect each SDR's inbox, and give the private app the email, call, sequence-enrolment and meeting-link scopes (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`). For phase 3, a second sequence whose step one is an automated email of the same two tokens, its ID in `AUXO_TIER2_SEQUENCE_ID`; leave it unset until the experiment is wanted.
7. Put the SDR queue in a HubSpot CRM card or a saved view sorted by `auxo_rank` with `auxo_reason` as a column; the service's pages do the same standalone.
