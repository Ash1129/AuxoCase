## Architecture, step by step

Shape: Fixed pipeline.

| Step | Name | Kind | Model or method | Hands on to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | New scored lead arrives | Start | Event trigger | 2 |
| 2 | Lead has the fields it needs? | Decision | Validation rules | 4 (valid), 3 (invalid) |
| 3 | Flag incomplete lead | Fixed step | Scripted action | 5 |
| 4 | Read SDR roster and open leads | Fixed step | Scripted action | 6 |
| 5 | Lead flagged for data fix | Finish | Operations queue | end |
| 6 | Find next SDR under capacity | Decision | Decision table | 7 (SDR found), 8 (all at capacity) |
| 7 | Set lead owner and advance turn | Fixed step | Scripted action | 9 |
| 8 | Hold lead for manager | Fixed step | Scripted action | 10 |
| 9 | Create task to alert SDR | Fixed step | Scripted action | 11 |
| 10 | Manager assigns held leads | Person | Sales manager review | 11 |
| 11 | Lead routed to an SDR | Finish | In HubSpot | end |

### 1. New scored lead arrives

- What happens: A lead receives its score in HubSpot and starts a run automatically.
- Why: You said the work should start automatically when something happens, and a newly scored lead is the event that needs routing.
- Passes on: The lead's record ID, score and the fields it was saved with.

### 2. Lead has the fields it needs?

- What happens: Confirms the lead has a contact ID, a numeric score and a territory or region value. A lead missing any of these cannot be routed safely.
- Why: A lead without these fields cannot be matched to the right SDR. Catching the gap here stops a wrong or blank owner being written to the record.
- Passes on: Valid leads go on to routing. Invalid leads go to the flag path, with the missing fields listed.
- Stop rule: Failures alert a person.

### 3. Flag incomplete lead

- What happens: Adds a note listing the missing fields and sets a 'needs data fix' property on the lead, so operations can correct it.
- Why: The lead is kept visible and not lost, and nobody is assigned on incomplete data.
- Passes on: The flagged lead ID and the list of missing fields.

### 4. Read SDR roster and open leads

- What happens: Reads the active SDR list, each SDR's current count of open leads, their capacity limit, and the round-robin position from the last assignment. Fetches every page of results.
- Why: Capacity is counted as open leads at any one time, so the counts must be current. Reading every page avoids missing SDRs who sit past the first page.
- Passes on: The SDR roster in round-robin order, with each SDR's open-lead count and limit, plus the lead.

### 5. Lead flagged for data fix

- What happens: The lead is left unassigned with a note listing what is missing, for correction.
- Why: Incomplete leads are fixed at source rather than routed wrongly.
- Passes on: Nothing further.

### 6. Find next SDR under capacity

- What happens: Starting after the last SDR assigned, walks the roster in order and picks the first SDR whose open-lead count is below their limit. If nobody is under the limit, it reports that all SDRs are at capacity.
- Why: Round-robin spreads leads evenly, and the capacity check stops any SDR being overloaded. Both rules are fixed, so the outcome is predictable.
- Passes on: The chosen SDR and the lead, or an all-at-capacity result with the lead.
- Stop rule: Failures alert a person.

### 7. Set lead owner and advance turn

- What happens: Sets the chosen SDR as the lead's owner and moves the round-robin position to that SDR. Before writing, it checks the lead has no owner yet, so a retry cannot assign it twice.
- Why: A single write with a check prevents duplicate assignments and keeps the rotation fair even if a run is repeated.
- Passes on: The lead ID, the new owner and the updated round-robin position.

### 8. Hold lead for manager

- What happens: Leaves the lead without an owner, marks it 'held: all SDRs at capacity' and records the time, so it appears in the manager's queue.
- Why: A lead that cannot be assigned must not be dropped or given to someone over their limit.
- Passes on: The held lead ID and the time it was held.

### 9. Create task to alert SDR

- What happens: Creates a HubSpot task assigned to the SDR, naming the lead and its score, so it appears in their task list.
- Why: You asked for the SDR to be alerted by a HubSpot task, and this puts the alert where they already work.
- Passes on: The task ID and the SDR it was assigned to.

### 10. Manager assigns held leads

- What happens: The sales manager reviews the held queue, assigns leads when SDRs have capacity, or raises a limit with a reason.
- Why: When every SDR is full, a person decides, and nothing is assigned past a limit without someone accountable for it.
- Passes on: Each held lead with an owner or a deliberate decision to wait.
- Person steps in: Manager clears the held queue.

### 11. Lead routed to an SDR

- What happens: The lead has an SDR owner and an alert task. Each run, and each failure, is logged so you can review it.
- Why: This is the outcome the task exists to produce, and the log shows the rotation is working.
- Passes on: Nothing further.

## Watch out for

- **Parallel runs can overfill an SDR.** If two leads are scored at nearly the same time, both runs may read the same open count and assign the last free slot to the same SDR. Serialise the routing step, and recheck the count and the owner just before each write. (knowledge base: N03)
- **Owner set but SDR never alerted.** The design sets the owner and then creates the task. If the task step fails, the SDR owns a lead they don't know about. Treat the routing as complete only when both the owner and the task exist, and have the run retry or flag the gap. (knowledge base: N03, N02)
- **Error alerts never tested with real failures.** A manual test run does not exercise the error workflow. Force a real automatic failure in a test workflow and confirm the alert arrives before going live, or failures could go unnoticed. (knowledge base: N02)
- **Held leads can sit unseen.** If the held queue is only a view nobody checks, leads go cold while the SDRs are full. Give the manager a scheduled prompt or count of held leads, and make clearing the queue a named responsibility, not an assumption. (knowledge base: A02, A03)
- **Don't add AI just because you can.** If the output is a fixed function of the input, AI adds cost and unpredictability. Vendors may pitch an AI agent for this; a plain workflow is the better buy.
- **Design the execution boundary.** A framework alone does not provide a secure sandbox. Define authentication, per-request isolation, persistent recovery state, schema and value validation, timeouts and retry limits. For generated code, restrict filesystem and network access, keep credentials outside the sandbox and limit compute. The starter does not implement these production controls.
- **You don't need MCP here.** MCP is how an AI discovers and uses tools. A workflow without AI uses the workflow tool's normal app connections, which are simpler to set up and audit.
- **n8n isn't open source.** n8n's licence lets you use and change it for your own internal business purposes. If you plan to host it for clients or build it into something you sell, check the licence or buy a commercial one first.
