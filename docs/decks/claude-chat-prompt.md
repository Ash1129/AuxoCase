THE PROBLEM (the brief): BrightPath is B2B payroll SaaS for 50 to 500-employee companies. Marketing creates ~6,000 leads a month from six channels; HubSpot's points model passes ~1,500 MQLs to 12 SDRs who can work only 500 and choose by gut feel, so ~1,000 are never contacted. Median first response is 3 days; a lead contacted within a day converts about twice as often. Partner referrals and webinar attendees close at 2 to 3x the average but score low because their forms leave job title and company size blank. Budget $100k to $300k, three months to go live. Sales is nervous about hallucinations; SDRs must keep the final call.

WHAT THE SYSTEM DOES: reads HubSpot continuously, gives every lead one calibrated probability of booking a demo, a rank in the pool, a plain-English reason, assigns it to an SDR within capacity and alerts them via a HubSpot task, writes score/rank/reason/routing back to the contact, and feeds bookings and closes back into a monthly retrain that goes live by fixed rules (or two signatures). Five AI steps take chores off the SDR: follow-ups sent for them, replies triaged, call notes written up, likely skips held for the manager, demos booked for them. No AI contacts a prospect; the reader must quote the record and can move a score at most 10 points.

HEADLINE RESULTS (before = 12 months of history, after = 2 simulated months with every AI step on, same 12 SDRs):
- Demos per 1,000 leads: 28.6 -> 108.2 (+278%)
- Demos booked a month: 172 -> 649 (+477)
- Leads worked a month: 497 -> 1,128
- Median hours from lead arrival to first touch: 70 -> 10
- Touched within 24 hours: 21% -> 82%

REVENUE: a demo is worth about $4,800 first-year contract value ($24,000 deal at 20% close; outcomes show 23% of 3,434 demos closed). 172 demos = $826k a month; 649 = $3.12M a month; +$2.29M a month, about +$27.5M a year. Cost per demo in SDR time $26; 1.72 demos per SDR hour.

SDR TIME: an SDR gives leads 20.8 hours a month. A lead cost 30 minutes (20 cold touch + two 5-minute follow-ups); with the AI steps 14.6 minutes. Capacity 500 -> 1,031 leads a month (42 -> 86 a desk). About 15 minutes saved a lead, ~290 SDR hours a month on 1,128 leads, ~24 hours per SDR, reinvested in leads never called. Capacity with one step off: follow-ups 514, call notes 810, skips 935, booking 850; a triaged reply takes 4 minutes instead of 10.

PICKS AND TRUST: ranked leads 60% booked (2,711 of 4,497), random exploration slice 20% (48 of 235), random below HubSpot's gate 27% (36 of 135). Calibration: when it says 55%, 54% book (bands: score 26% -> 12% booked, 36 -> 34, 46 -> 46, 55 -> 54, 64 -> 64, 74 -> 77); expected calibration error 0.014 on 1,652 test leads.

TECHNICAL (CTO deck): one Python service (FastAPI, Jinja2, SQLite, scikit-learn, httpx), three jobs: score, route, learn; a mock HubSpot with the real API's semantics (duplicated/reordered webhooks, batch writes with per-record status, compare-and-set owner); real v3 client written from the docs but not yet run on a live portal; 175 offline tests. Scoring pipeline: enrich -> 17 features (channel, seniority, size band, industry, country; page views, visits, forms, pricing views, email clicks, webinar registered/attended, referral, competitor, in target size, student title, has notes; no names, emails or note text) -> calibrated logistic regression (gradient-boosted challenger) -> Claude reads notes for buying signals with verbatim quotes, capped at +/-10 points -> reason codes from exact contributions -> review flag -> rank -> batch write auxo_* properties keyed by contact + model + input hash. Training: time-ordered split, fit on oldest months, choose family and Platt-calibrate on next two, test once on newest two, ship the tested model; labels booked / worked-no-demo / not-worked (unlabelled); metrics precision@K, lift, AUC-PR, ECE, never accuracy. Versions (lift over HubSpot's points / over the cold-start points table / ECE): v1 1.196/1.008/0.007; v2 1.222/0.928/0.031; v3 1.155/0.991/0.050; v4 1.126/0.945/0.027; v5 1.249/0.964/0.053; v6 live 1.234/0.988/0.014 (precision 0.690 at K 481 vs HubSpot 0.559; 5,595 labelled rows); v7 1.260/1.034/0.127 sent back (numbers off by 12.7 points). Routing: pool = unowned MQLs under 30 days, ordered by p(demo) x 0.5 past the first day; team holds at most a week's reach, round robin, 8% random exploration slice; task due in 4 h, reminder at 24 h; unplaced strong leads and likely skips go to the manager's held queue. Capacity = SDRs x hours / minutes a lead; minutes a cold touch and hours a month are measured from the SDRs' own first touches once there are 30 gaps across 2 SDRs and 10 active days. Learning: hourly outcome sync, daily per-channel p-chart (observed vs predicted demo rate, 3 sigma, under 30 held), monthly retrain; sign-off rules: send back if calibration error more than 1 point worse than live, approve if lift better and at least as many rows, else send back; Claude words the verdict in one sentence checked by code (verdict first, under 60 words, no modelling jargon, only the facts' figures); automatic sign-off promotes the best approvable candidate. Jobs on timers: score 5 min, rescore 1, route 5, touches 1, outreach 1, followups 5, flags 10, remind 60, sync 60, monitor daily, train 30 days. Security: three roles (SDR, sales manager, marketing), scrypt passwords with lockout, HttpOnly hashed sessions, allowed hosts, origin checks, CSP, stale-edit tokens, signed webhooks. Known gaps: real client untested on a live portal; all figures synthetic and the first-day effect is the generator's assumption from BrightPath's answer; measured capacity rests on 11 touches; on worked leads the model barely beats the channel-aware points table until the exploration slice has run for months.
