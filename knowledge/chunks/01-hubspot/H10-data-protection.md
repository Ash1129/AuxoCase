## Claims

- Turning on HubSpot's data privacy settings (Super Admin or Edit account defaults) enables the cookie consent banner, adds data-privacy sections to new forms, enables unsubscribe links for one-to-one and sequence emails, flags contacts lacking a legal basis in the Sales extension, and disables email open and click tracking for contacts without a legal basis value; EU-hosted accounts have the settings on by default [hubspot-kb-gdpr-settings §Turn on data privacy settings].
- The contact property "Legal basis for processing contact's data" has options Legitimate interest – Lead, Legitimate interest – Customer, Legitimate interest – Other, Performance of a contract, Freely given consent from contact, and Not applicable; it can be set by import, on creation, bulk edit, individual edit, subscription changes and workflows, and requires Edit contacts permission [hubspot-kb-legal-basis §View the property and understand each legal basis].
- Subscription types require both a legal basis for communicating and a legal basis for processing, and opted-out contacts can be resubscribed only with a legal basis and an explanation [hubspot-kb-legal-basis §Grant legal basis for contacts].
- Setting the legal basis to "Not applicable" exempts the contact from GDPR protections in HubSpot [hubspot-kb-contact-properties §Ownership, dates, and compliance].
- A restorable delete allows restoration within 90 days; a permanent delete is available to all accounts and is intended for GDPR and similar laws [hubspot-kb-gdpr-settings §Delete contacts].
- Permanent delete removes the contact record and its associations to engagements (email tracking, calls, form submissions and other activity), can take up to 30 days to purge, requires "Permanently delete contacts" permission, works only on individual records (not segments or workflows), and leaves anonymised analytics in reports; the email is blocklisted so it cannot be re-added by UI or import, and a later re-creation gets a new record with no history [hubspot-kb-gdpr-delete §Understand the data purged after a permanent delete].
- The API equivalent is `POST /crm/v3/objects/contacts/gdpr-delete` with `objectId` and optional `idProperty` (for example `email`), returning 204; if an email is given and not found it is added to the blocklist [hubspot-docs-gdpr-delete §Permanently delete a contact (GDPR-compliant)].
- A `contact.privacyDeletion` webhook subscription exists, and privacy-deletion notifications may arrive in separate batches, so they must be matched by object ID [hubspot-docs-webhooks §Privacy compliant contact deletions].
- Properties can be flagged as sensitive (Enterprise beta) and then require the `dataSensitivity=sensitive` parameter to be read, and `propertyChange` webhook payloads carry an `isSensitive` flag [hubspot-docs-properties §Retrieve properties; hubspot-docs-webhooks-projects §Response payloads].
- Under UK PECR, sole traders and some partnerships are treated as individuals (consent or soft opt-in needed) while any corporate body can be emailed, but emailing named employees at corporate addresses still raises data protection considerations, a valid opt-out address is mandatory, and the guidance is under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act [ico-pecr-email §When can we email or text businesses?].
- The ICO says profiling for direct marketing must be fair, must be explained to people, must use information that is accurate and "not excessive", becomes more intrusive with more or more sensitive data, and obtaining extra contact details from other sources without consent is likely unfair; buyers of third-party lead lists must do proportionate due diligence [ico-dm-leads §Can we create profiles of people for direct marketing?].
- The ICO describes profiling as any form of automated processing used to analyse, evaluate or predict aspects of a person's behaviour or interests, and says the automated decision-making provisions apply only when a decision is solely automated (no meaningful human involvement) and has legal or similarly significant effects; profiling is often invisible to people and carries statistical accuracy and bias risks [ico-adm-profiling §What is profiling?].
- Under the CCPA, personal information is information that identifies, relates to or could reasonably be linked with a consumer or household; the business-to-business and employee exemptions expired on December 31, 2022; consumers have rights to know, delete, correct, opt out of sale/sharing, limit sensitive-data use and non-discrimination, with 45-day response windows extendable once [ca-oag-ccpa §A. General information about the CCPA].

## Implications for the build

- Treat every contact record as personal data under both GDPR and CCPA (the CCPA B2B exemption is gone), and document the scorer as processing under the same legal basis HubSpot records: read `hs_legal_basis` and skip or down-weight contacts whose basis is empty, in line with how HubSpot itself suspends tracking for them.
- Minimise what leaves HubSpot: the model needs behavioural counts, timestamps, firmographics, source and stage; it does not need names, phone numbers, email bodies, notes or free-text form answers. Pull those fields never, and pseudonymise the record ID in the service's feature store.
- The scorer ranks leads for human SDRs; keep it that way (humans decide whom to contact and how), state it in the DPIA, and never let the score alone trigger a consequential automated action such as refusing service, so it stays outside solely-automated decision rules.
- Handle deletion end to end: subscribe to `contact.privacyDeletion`, delete the service's stored features and logs for that `objectId` within the same 30-day window HubSpot uses, and never call `gdpr-delete` with an email guess, because a miss blocklists the address.
- Do not enrich from third-party sources inside the scorer without a documented basis and due diligence; use the firmographics already in the CRM's primary company.
- Keep the plain-English reason factual and non-sensitive (for example "visited pricing twice this week; 120-employee company; webinar attended"), because it is stored on the record and could be disclosed in a subject access request.
